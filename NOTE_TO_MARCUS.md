# Note to Marcus

Marcus,

**What you'll see on Monday.** Open the tracker and the first screen is your briefing. At the top is one sentence saying where we stand. Below it are four score cards (us, Trakvia, Routelyne, Gridwell), each with a small six-week trend line. Then you'll see:

- **what changed and why**
- **who gained or lost ground**, and on which AI engine
- **suggested actions**
- **wrong-fact alerts**, covering us and, as sales ammunition, our competitors

Every alert links to the actual AI answer, so you can read exactly what a prospect would have read.

**How the score works.** Every week we ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews the 15 questions our buyers ask, twice each. In each answer, a company earns:

- **100 points** if the AI recommends it
- **50 points** if it is just mentioned
- **25 points** if it is criticised
- **nothing** if the AI tells people to avoid it or leaves it out

The score is the average out of 100, with our most important questions counting most. In plain terms: *when our buyers ask AI for advice, how well does it treat us?*

**Why this score.** It is simple enough to explain in a sentence, and it rewards what actually wins deals. Being recommended is worth twice as much as merely being named. It also treats competitors exactly the same way, so the numbers are directly comparable.

**Why it sometimes says "noise".** AI tools give different answers to the same question minutes apart, and our two runs often disagree. The tracker measures that wobble and only calls a change "real" when it is clearly bigger than the wobble. One week is rarely enough to be sure, so it also compares the last two weeks with the two before, and looks at each AI engine separately. When a week's data is incomplete (Perplexity is missing from week 5), it says so and does not show a false drop.

**What it shows right now (week 6).** We score 38, second to Trakvia's 47. Overall the change is within normal variation, but one real problem stands out. On **Google AI Overviews** our score has fallen from about 45 to 21 over the last two weeks, and **Routelyne has taken that ground**. AI tools are also telling buyers some wrong things about us:

- that we're headquartered in Chicago, or in Columbus, Georgia
- that we were founded in 2009
- that we don't integrate with QuickBooks
- that we sell dashcams

Our pages and review-site profiles should state the true facts plainly. Meanwhile, AI is telling buyers Trakvia costs $25 (it's $39) and that Routelyne offers ELD compliance (it doesn't). Those are useful points for the sales team.

**What it can't do.** It reads answers with fixed rules rather than understanding them the way a person does. On a hand-checked sample it was right every time, but on new wording it will sometimes miss a nuance. Every number links back to the original answer, so you can always check for yourself.
